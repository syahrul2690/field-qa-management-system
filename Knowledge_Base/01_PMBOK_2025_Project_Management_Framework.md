## Overview & Scope

The PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition is the premier global standard for project management practice. This edition represents a fundamental evolution in how project management is structured and communicated, reflecting the dynamic needs of modern organizations across industries, sectors, and development methodologies.

### Key Strategic Shifts in Eighth Edition

- **Evidence-Based Design:** Shaped by nearly 48,000 data points and global practitioner insights
- **Accessibility Focus:** Updated definitions aligned to current global practice and language accessibility
- **Flexible Methodology:** Adaptable practices suitable for diverse industries, development approaches, and organizational contexts
- **Value-Centric Delivery:** Emphasis on value delivery, not just output execution

## Project Management Principles

### Six Actionable Principles (Refined from Previous 12)

The Eighth Edition consolidates project management principles to be more actionable and minimize overlap and confusion. These six principles guide effective practice:

1. **Adopt a Holistic View**
   - Consider project impact across organizational and stakeholder systems
   - Integrate technical, business, and human factors
   - Connected Performance Domains: Governance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk

2. **Focus on Value**
   - Align project objectives with organizational strategy
   - Maximize value delivery to stakeholders
   - Connected Performance Domains: Governance, Scope, Finance, Stakeholders

3. **Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables**
   - Quality is built in, not inspected in
   - Integrate quality considerations throughout project lifecycle
   - Connected Performance Domains: Scope, Schedule, Resources, Risk

4. **Be an Accountable Leader**
   - Demonstrate integrity, responsibility, and ethical behavior
   - Foster accountability across project teams
   - Connected Performance Domains: Governance, Stakeholders, Resources

5. **Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas**
   - Balance environmental, social, and economic considerations
   - Embed sustainability in decision-making processes
   - Connected Performance Domains: Governance, Finance, Resources, Risk

6. **Build an Empowered Culture**
   - Enable team members to make decisions
   - Foster collaborative, inclusive work environment
   - Connected Performance Domains: Stakeholders, Resources

## Project Management Focus Areas

### Five Focus Areas (Reimagined Process Groups)

Historically presented as formal logical categories known as Process Groups, the Eighth Edition reimagines these as Focus Areas to reflect how modern projects satisfy these concepts through multiple approaches—formal processes, informal practices, and flexible policies.

### 1. **Initiating Focus Area**

**Purpose:** Authorize and define the project or phase

**Key Activities:**
- Project charter development
- Authorization and sponsorship
- Stakeholder identification
- High-level requirements definition
- Project success criteria establishment

### 2. **Planning Focus Area**

**Purpose:** Establish project scope, objectives, and strategy

**Key Activities:**
- Comprehensive project plan development
- Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) creation
- Schedule development and resource planning
- Risk identification and planning
- Quality planning and stakeholder engagement strategy

### 3. **Executing Focus Area**

**Purpose:** Perform work to accomplish project objectives

**Key Activities:**
- Direct and manage project work
- Quality assurance activities
- Team development and management
- Procurement execution
- Stakeholder engagement and communication

### 4. **Monitoring and Controlling Focus Area**

**Purpose:** Track and manage project performance

**Key Activities:**
- Project performance monitoring
- Change control and management
- Variance analysis and corrective actions
- Risk monitoring and response
- Quality assurance and scope verification

### 5. **Closing Focus Area**

**Purpose:** Formally close project or phase

**Key Activities:**
- Final deliverable verification
- Project or phase closure procedures
- Lessons learned documentation
- Resource release and transition
- Project archive and knowledge transfer

## Project Management Performance Domains

### Seven Comprehensive Performance Domains

The Eighth Edition synthesizes traditional Knowledge Areas with other accepted concepts into seven performance domains. Each domain integrates key concepts and includes nonprescriptive processes adaptable to varied approaches, life cycles, and environments.

### 1. **Governance Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Oversight, decision-making, and strategic alignment

**Key Concepts:**
- Project value creation and realization
- Governance models and structures
- Decision-making frameworks
- Performance metrics and monitoring mechanisms
- Organizational governance connection

**Primary Processes:**
- Develop Project Charter
- Define Governance Framework
- Manage Project Governance
- Monitor and Control Project Work (governance aspect)

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Organizational structure and culture alignment
- Regulatory and compliance requirements
- Stakeholder authority and decision rights
- Project complexity and risk profile

**Key Interactions:** Links with all other domains to ensure strategic alignment

---

### 2. **Scope Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Definition and verification of project work boundaries

**Key Concepts:**
- Scope statement and product description
- Requirements definition and management
- Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)
- Scope validation and change control
- Deliverable definition and acceptance

**Primary Processes:**
- Plan Scope Management
- Collect Requirements
- Define Scope
- Validate Scope
- Control Scope

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Requirements definition approach (prescriptive vs. adaptive)
- Scope change frequency and management
- Customer involvement level
- Documentation detail requirements

**Key Interactions:** Affects Schedule, Resources, Finance, Quality, and Risk Domains

---

### 3. **Schedule Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Time-based project execution planning and control

**Key Concepts:**
- Schedule development methodologies
- Activity definition and sequencing
- Duration estimation
- Schedule optimization and baseline
- Progress tracking and variance management

**Primary Processes:**
- Plan Schedule Management
- Define Activities
- Sequence Activities
- Estimate Activity Durations
- Develop Schedule
- Control Schedule

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Scheduling methodology (Gantt, Critical Path, Agile)
- Level of detail in schedule hierarchy
- Dependencies and constraint handling
- Schedule risk and buffer strategies

**Key Interactions:** Dependent on Scope; affects Resources, Finance, Risk Domains

---

### 4. **Finance Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Budget development, cost management, and financial control

**Key Concepts:**
- Project budgeting and cost estimation
- Financial planning and tracking
- Value earned and performance indices
- Budget baseline and variance analysis
- Financial risk management

**Primary Processes:**
- Plan Cost Management
- Estimate Costs
- Determine Budget
- Control Costs

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Cost estimation accuracy requirements
- Funding constraints and allocation mechanisms
- Earned Value Management (EVM) applicability
- Financial reporting frequency and detail

**Key Interactions:** Linked with Scope, Schedule, Procurement, Risk Domains

---

### 5. **Stakeholders Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Identification, analysis, and engagement of project stakeholders

**Key Concepts:**
- Stakeholder identification and analysis
- Engagement strategy development
- Communication planning and execution
- Trust and satisfaction management
- Conflict resolution and influence

**Primary Processes:**
- Identify Stakeholders
- Plan Stakeholder Engagement
- Manage Stakeholder Engagement
- Monitor Stakeholder Engagement

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Stakeholder complexity and diversity
- Communication frequency and formality
- Cultural and language considerations
- Power, interest, and impact analysis depth

**Key Interactions:** Critical to all domains; especially Governance, Resources, Risk

---

### 6. **Resources Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Acquisition, allocation, and management of project resources

**Key Concepts:**
- Resource planning and estimation
- Team composition and competencies
- Resource availability and allocation
- Performance management and development
- Physical and material resources

**Primary Processes:**
- Plan Resource Management
- Estimate Activity Resources
- Acquire Resources
- Develop Team
- Manage Team
- Control Resources

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Resource availability and constraints
- Team structure and co-location options
- Competency development requirements
- Resource leveling and scheduling

**Key Interactions:** Constrained by Schedule, Finance; enables Quality and Risk management

---

### 7. **Risk Performance Domain**

**Scope:** Identification, analysis, and response to project uncertainties

**Key Concepts:**
- Risk identification and categorization
- Qualitative and quantitative analysis
- Risk response planning (avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept)
- Risk monitoring and control
- Opportunity management

**Primary Processes:**
- Plan Risk Management
- Identify Risks
- Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis
- Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis
- Plan Risk Responses
- Monitor Risks

**Tailoring Considerations:**
- Risk appetite and tolerance levels
- Regulatory and compliance risk drivers
- Analysis depth and formality required
- Organizational risk maturity

**Key Interactions:** Integrates with all domains; fundamental to Governance and decision-making

## Project Life Cycles and Development Approaches

### Project Phases

Projects are organized into phases that provide a logical structure for managing work:

**Phase Characteristics:**
- Distinct set of activities
- Defined deliverables
- Milestone completion points
- Resource and funding transitions
- Gate reviews and control points

### Development Approaches

#### **Predictive Approaches**
- Comprehensive upfront planning
- Requirements defined at project start
- Changes managed through formal control processes
- Suitable for: Clear requirements, stable scope, regulated environments

#### **Adaptive Approaches**
- Iterative and incremental delivery
- Requirements evolve over project duration
- Frequent delivery cycles and feedback loops
- Suitable for: Uncertain/evolving requirements, fast-changing environments

#### **Hybrid Approaches**
- Combination of predictive and adaptive elements
- Typical in modern infrastructure and complex projects
- Early phases may be predictive; delivery phases adaptive

### Considerations for Development Approach Selection

**Deliverables Factors:**
- Clarity of requirements
- Stakeholder involvement capability
- Frequency of deliverable changes

**Project Factors:**
- Complexity and novelty
- Schedule and budget constraints
- Organizational capability

**Organization Factors:**
- Culture and process maturity
- Available expertise and resources
- Governance and compliance requirements

## Delivery Cadence

The frequency and timing of deliverable releases and value realization:

- **Continuous Delivery:** Frequent incremental releases
- **Regular Cadence:** Scheduled release cycles (weekly, monthly, quarterly)
- **Phase-Gate Releases:** Major releases at project phase boundaries
- **One-Time Delivery:** Single comprehensive delivery at project completion

## Key Terms and Foundational Concepts

### Project Characteristics
- **Temporary:** Defined beginning and end
- **Unique:** Distinct from operations and other projects
- **Progressive Elaboration:** Characteristics refined over project duration
- **Value Creation:** Delivers benefits to organization and stakeholders

### Organizational Governance and Project Governance Connection

Project governance must connect to and support organizational governance:
- Strategic alignment with portfolio and program management
- Compliance with organizational policies and procedures
- Risk management integration
- Stakeholder authority and decision rights

### Relationship of Portfolio, Program, Project, and Operations Management

| Dimension | Portfolio | Program | Project | Operations |
|-----------|-----------|---------|---------|------------|
| **Scope** | Strategic initiatives | Related projects | Temporary deliverable | Ongoing services |
| **Duration** | Ongoing | Multiple project cycles | Defined start/end | Continuous |
| **Governance** | Strategic oversight | Program coordination | Project delivery | Operational management |
| **Management Focus** | Value optimization | Interdependency | Scope/schedule/budget | Efficiency/quality |

## Project Value Delivery Components

### Creating Value

Value is multidimensional and includes:
- **Financial:** Revenue, profit, return on investment
- **Strategic:** Market position, capability, competitive advantage
- **Social:** Impact on stakeholders and community
- **Environmental:** Sustainability and resource management
- **Functional:** Features and performance of deliverables

### Assessing Project Success

Success criteria extend beyond traditional triple constraint (scope, time, cost):
- **Deliverable Quality:** Meets specifications and standards
- **Stakeholder Satisfaction:** Expectations met or exceeded
- **Business Value Realization:** Intended benefits achieved
- **Organizational Capability:** Enhanced organizational competencies
- **Sustainability:** Positive long-term impact

## Technical Glossary and Key Definitions

### Core Terms (PMBOK 2025 Eighth Edition)

**Activity:** Work element performed as part of a project

**Budget:** Approved cost estimate assigned to schedule activities

**Change Request:** Formal proposal to modify project documents, processes, or scope

**Configuration Management:** Systematic process for coordinating changes to project deliverables

**Deliverable:** Unique, verifiable product, result, or capability to be produced

**Earned Value Management (EVM):** Method of measuring project performance

**Governance:** Oversight and decision-making mechanisms for strategic alignment

**Influence:** Ability to affect decisions and outcomes through relationships and positioning

**Lessons Learned:** Knowledge and insights gained during project execution

**Performance Domain:** Integrated set of project management practices and competencies

**Predictive Approach:** Development approach based on comprehensive upfront planning

**Process Group (Historical):** Now reimagined as Focus Areas in Eighth Edition

**Progressive Elaboration:** Continuous refinement of project characteristics throughout lifecycle

**Risk Response:** Actions taken to address identified risks

**Scope:** All work required to complete project objectives

**Stakeholder:** Individual or organization impacted by or able to impact the project

**Value:** Benefits and outcomes delivered to the organization and stakeholders
